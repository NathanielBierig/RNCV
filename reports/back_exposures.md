# Whole back exposures (exploratory)

74 attempts grouped into **65 back exposures** (a new exposure starts after an escape or 30 s without an attempt). Generated by `research/model/exposures.py`. Exploratory: small numbers, finish-enriched sample, and time on the back without a choke attempt isn't labeled.

| Leg control at the first attempt | Exposures | Finished (any attempt) | 95% interval | Attempts per exposure (mean) | Seconds on the neck per exposure (median) | Exposure span, s (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| body triangle | 17 | 10 (59%) | 36–78% | 1.3 | 12.2 | 12.2 |
| both hooks | 25 | 13 (52%) | 33–70% | 1.1 | 10.6 | 10.6 |
| one hook / none | 23 | 17 (74%) | 54–87% | 1.1 | 9.3 | 9.3 |

Exposures with more than one attempt: **9 of 65**. Finish rate when the attacker got 2+ attempts: 4/9; with a single attempt: 36/56.

**How to read it:** if leg control buys retention, body-triangle and both-hooks exposures should show *more attempts* and *more seconds on the neck* per exposure, and their exposure-level finish rate should catch up with or pass the one-hook/none group, even though their per-attempt rate is lower ([findings_pilot.md](findings_pilot.md)). With this few exposures, differences within the 95% intervals are not evidence either way.
